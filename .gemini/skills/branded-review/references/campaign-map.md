# Visual campaign structure

The default report includes this proposal as its second tab, even without Merch Jar. For a separately requested sketch, generate `scripts/campaign_map.py --html-output ABSOLUTE_OUTPUT` and deliver its verified report artifact. Lead with three plain-language steps on one page: keep the existing campaigns running, add new branded campaigns, then use reviewed negatives to keep the existing campaigns for non-branded traffic after the branded campaigns are delivering. Show own-product defense as its own colored box inside both steps 2 and 3, and the reusable negative list below. Its delivery check is independent from branded searches. The first glance should explain what changes and what stays. Avoid labels such as destination architecture, scope, roles, gates and legacy eligibility in the customer visual.

Prefer new branded campaigns plus retained existing non-brand campaigns for mixed accounts. This is a proposed approach; identify actual exceptions from the connected review. Useful auto, broad, phrase and product targeting can remain within the non-brand part without becoming four extra boxes in the overview. Keep own-product defense separate from brand searches and competitor products. Unknown ASINs remain unknown.

To explain an untidy starting point, pass `--analysis PRIVATE/analysis.json`. The visual shows per-campaign branded/non-brand spend and ACoS, ASIN spend and total spend. It starts with the ten campaigns receiving the most branded spend, with search, total-spend sorting, a mixed-traffic filter and more rows. All campaigns remain available; product counts, IDs and traffic labels are secondary detail. Keep it in a disclosure, use wrapping/stacked mobile rows and show historical dates. This is historical traffic, not current configuration; names do not establish product groups. Private names and IDs require a separate fictional or privacy presentation for recording.

Use [product-structure.md](product-structure.md) to populate the full product proposal. The dropdowns show campaign budgets, category/variation/product grouping, actual targets and advertised products. Counts change with the chosen structure; held products remain visible. Show actual saved brand names and ASINs in both planning tabs. Setup & progress separates historical/current evidence, proposed new coverage and next actions. The other-product callout explains retaining coverage and conditional separation; do not universally prescribe competitor campaigns.

Keep category colors consistent with Performance: muted teal for brand searches, warm stone for other searches, slate for owned-product defense, ink for totals and lime for takeaways. Align the six cards in columns 2 and 3 at equal height on desktop, retaining natural height on mobile. Consolidate supporting negative-list and transition explanations below the main diagram in one disclosure; do not repeat the defense explanation in several full sections.

In the connected current-state map, group by verified advertised product families where possible; keep “multiple product groups” and “products not mapped yet” groups when needed. Summarize each group with the campaign count and traffic mix, then let the user inspect actual campaign names and IDs. Show each campaign's planned change in the same row: keep for non-brand, keep branded, separate own-product defense, or review. Do not draw every keyword as a box or invent a tidy current hierarchy. Retain all campaigns in the inventory and disclose unsupported/unmapped scope.

In connected planning, build the account-specific map from verified current entities and the reviewed plan: existing campaign/ad-group IDs, product scope, reuse/new/retain decisions, proposed destinations, proposed exclusions and delivery gates. Do not imply the generic map contains these facts. Include goal/budget choices only once supported and clearly distinguish proposed from current values. A campaign map is never an execution manifest or account-write approval.

Keep planning and visual explanation conceptually separate from Create Campaigns. The planner owns structure, product coverage, migration and tradeoffs; visualization displays that plan. Naming supplies the agreed naming convention. Create Campaigns validates and executes the approved concrete manifest. A later reusable planning skill can own the shared map without expanding this release into a general account-rebuild engine.
